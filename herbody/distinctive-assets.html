herbody · distinctive assets · round 3

A layout is not a brand.

Every render so far is a coloured pouch, a wordmark and a spec strip. Competent, and interchangeable. A brand is one thing you own that works without your name — a shape, a device, a word habit, a structural trick — that comes from a truth of the product and survives at 32 px. Here are four, each drawn, each tested at favicon size, each with the reason it can't be borrowed by Wellgard next month.

Concept 01

The Scoop

"One scoop" is the entire proposition — so the scoop becomes the mark, the window and the object in the pouch.

the mark
32 px · 16 px
herbody
BODY
BEAUTY
5gCREATINE · 5gCOLLAGEN · 24VITS & MINS · INOSITOL
One scoop · 30 servings · 384g ℮
The asset
A circle-and-handle glyph. On pack it is a real clear window the size of a scoop, showing the pink powder — the mark is literally the product. A copper scoop ships in every pouch.
Why ownable
Nobody on the UK grid owns the scoop; it answers the #2 review complaint (scoop and serving honesty) in one image; a window lifts purchase intent in the evidence; and a circle on a rectangle is a shape, which is what the eye finds first.
System
The glyph replaces the "o" in social handles, becomes the app icon and sticker, the pattern (scoops in rows), and the loading animation (a scoop filling). Copy habit: everything is "one scoop": "one scoop of strength", "one scoop, sorted".
Costs
Clear-window pouch adds converter cost; copper scoop adds ~£0.60–1.20 per unit. Both are the point.
WinsThe most literal, the most defensible, and the one that turns a packaging detail into a brand promise.
RisksWindow + berry powder must be tested for light stability; a bad window print looks cheap.
Concept 02

The Five

Five things in one scoop — creatine, collagen, vitamins, inositol, botanicals — so the mark is a five-petal asterisk, and the asterisk becomes the brand's punctuation.

the mark
32 px · 16 px
herbody
BODY
BEAUTY*
5gCREATINE · 5gCOLLAGEN · 24VITS & MINS · INOSITOL
*five in one · 30 servings · 384g ℮
The asset
A five-petal asterisk. It sits after the product name like a footnote — BODY BEAUTY* — and the asterisk always resolves to "*five in one". Each petal can be labelled on the back and in A+ content.
Why ownable
It encodes the wedge (five, not two) in a shape; an asterisk is a typographic habit competitors can't copy without looking like you; and five-fold symmetry reads as a flower without being floral.
System
The asterisk is the social avatar, the bullet in every list, the pattern on the insert card, the "loading" spinner, and a pin badge. Copy habit: claims end with the asterisk and the footnote — which is also a neat way to keep UK claims tidy.
Costs
None beyond a proper vector mark. Works on any pouch, any colour.
WinsCheapest to execute, scales into a range (each SKU keeps the asterisk, changes what it resolves to), and it makes the differentiation graphic.
RisksAsterisks also signal "small print"; the resolving footnote must be confident, never apologetic. Needs a hand-tuned drawing, not a stock glyph.
Concept 03

her.

The name already contains the idea — her body. Make "her" a stamp, and make every sentence the brand says start with it.

the mark
32 px · 16 px
her.
body
her body.
her beauty.
her scoop.
5gCREATINE · 5gCOLLAGEN · 24VITS & MINS · INOSITOL
Body Beauty · 30 servings · 384g ℮
The asset
A solid pill with her. reversed out — full stop included. It's the wordmark's first syllable promoted to a logo, so the brand name and the mark are the same thing.
Why ownable
It's registered — you own the word in the name. Nobody else can stamp "her." on a supplement without trading on you. And a language is harder to copy than a colour: "her strength", "her mornings", "her rules" — every headline for the next five years is written.
System
The chip is the avatar and sticker; the copy system is the brand: product pages, ads, packs and social all open with "her". The full stop is the tone — calm, decided. "Body Beauty" stays as the product name in the strip.
Costs
None. But it demands discipline: the day someone writes "unlock your glow-up" the system dies.
WinsTurns the trademark into the brand idea; the most "authored" of the four; ages best; perfect for the 35–55 buyer who wants to be addressed, not sold to.
Risks"her." carries a politics some buyers read as earnest; the pill can feel like a label unless the type is drawn well.
Concept 04

The Line

Your own render found it: the raspberry diagonal. Make it the only fixed thing — one line, always the same angle, across every pouch, image and post.

the mark
32 px · 16 px
herbody
BODY
BEAUTY
5gCREATINE · 5gCOLLAGEN · 24VITS & MINS · INOSITOL
Strawberry + Raspberry · 30 servings · 384g ℮
The asset
A single raspberry band at 32°, edge to edge. It never changes angle, weight or colour. Everything else — pouch colour, flavour, product — can.
Why ownable
Shelves and search grids are made of upright rectangles; a diagonal is the rarest shape there, and shape is what the eye finds first (eye-tracking evidence, §evidence). At 64 px it's still a diagonal when every word has gone.
System
The line crosses every hero image, cuts the corner of every social tile, underlines every headline, and animates as a wipe in video. Flavour range: same line, different ground. Copy habit: "the daily line" — the streak she keeps.
Costs
None; it's ink. Needs registration care where it crosses the gusset.
WinsStrongest at feed speed and thumbnail; the easiest to apply consistently with five hours a week; a genuine range system.
RisksThe least "meaningful" of the four — it's a mark of consistency, not of an idea — unless the copy gives it one (the streak, the line she holds).

The pick

Two of these combine; the other two are single bets

Recommendation

Concept 03 (her.) as the brand, Concept 01 (the Scoop) as the pack. "her." gives you the idea and the language; the scoop window gives the pouch an ownable shape and answers the category's honesty problem. Together they read: her. one scoop. — a brand you can say, draw, and hand someone. Keep the Five as the back-panel and A+ device (five petals, five pillars) and hold the Line for a future range system. If you want one bet only: the Scoop.

  • Why not just pick the prettiest render: prettiness is what image models are good at and what competitors can regenerate in an afternoon. A window, a stamp, a footnote habit — those have to be decided, and decisions are what a brand is.
  • What to spend real money on: one human designer, one week, £600–1,500, to draw the chosen mark properly (optical corrections, the "her." type, the scoop glyph proportions) and hand you a vector kit. Everything else can stay AI-generated against the brief.
  • What not to do: ask Gemini for "a unique brand". It will give you the category average with a gradient. Ask it, instead, for the specific asset below.

Concept 03 · social avatar and sticker

Logo mark: a horizontal rounded pill in black-cherry (#3A1A2B) with the word "her." in cream (#F4EDE6), extra-bold condensed grotesk, tight letter-spacing, the full stop the same weight as the letters. Show it as a 1:1 app icon on white, as a 3D embossed sticker on a matte pouch, and as a 16 px favicon. Flat vector, no shadows, no gradient, no extra symbols.

Concept 02 · the Five, for A+ content

A five-petal asterisk mark in raspberry (#C2255C) on cream, each petal a smooth elongated ellipse meeting at a small cream centre; beside it the text "BODY BEAUTY*" and the footnote "*five in one: creatine · collagen · vitamins & minerals · myo-inositol · botanicals", each petal labelled with one of the five. Flat vector, editorial, no gradients.
