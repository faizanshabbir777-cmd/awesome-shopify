# herbody — Amazon Launch Plan (1,300-unit first run)

A launch plan for a solo founder with a full-time job. Built for roughly **5 focused hours per week**. The theme throughout: with 1,300 units, this run's job is to *validate*, not to scale. Spend money and hours only on things that directly cause a sale or protect the launch.

---

## 1. The positioning call (make this once, it shapes everything)

"Be like AG1" is the right ambition but the wrong playbook for launch. AG1 grew on podcasts, founder authority, and DTC subscriptions — a model that takes years and millions. What you *can* take from AG1 is the **belief system**: "replace your shelf of bottles with one daily ritual."

Your version, sharpened for women and for Amazon search:

> **herbody — the all-in-one daily for women. Creatine, collagen, full-spectrum vitamins and inositol in one strawberry-raspberry scoop — without the bloat.**

Why "without the bloat" matters: your formula's most unusual pairing (creatine + a diuretic/water-balance blend) is either a weakness or your headline, depending on who names it first. The #1 objection women raise about creatine — check the negative reviews on any women's creatine listing — is water retention and bloating. You are one of the few products that answers it *inside the formula*. Own that.

Comparable brands to study (not copy): Bloom Nutrition (women's greens, TikTok-native, accessible price), Create (creatine gummies for a general audience), Mixhers (women's-cycle positioning), Ritual (transparency-led). Your gap: nobody owns "women's all-in-one **with clinically meaningful creatine**" on Amazon yet — greens powders sprinkle it or skip it.

**Wording care (do this before printing, see §2):** never print or write the word "diuretic" as a claim. Use structure/function language: "supports healthy water balance," "helps reduce the feeling of bloat." "Diuretic" invites drug-claim scrutiny from the FDA and Amazon's automated checks.

## 2. Week 0 — two gates before anything else

These two items block everything downstream. Do them before touching the listing, the website, or the charity idea.

### Gate A: label compliance check — BEFORE the pouch prints
You've finalized artwork but not printed. This is the single luckiest fact in your situation, because label mistakes on 1,300 printed pouches are unfixable. Have your co-packer (or a supplement label consultant, ~$300–600 on Upwork/regulatory firms) verify:

- Supplement Facts panel formatted per 21 CFR 101.36 (serving size, %DV, order of nutrients)
- FDA disclaimer present: *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*
- All claims are structure/function only ("supports energy," never "treats," "cures," "burns fat," "detox")
- Allergen statement, net weight, manufacturer/distributor line, country of origin
- No "diuretic," no weight-loss promises, no disease references
- Lot/expiry area exists on the pouch

### Gate A½: settle these with Black Label Creations BEFORE agreeing payment terms
You're waiting on payment terms — that's leverage, and it disappears the moment you pay. Fold these into the same conversation (one email, this week):

1. **cGMP proof** — ask for their current cGMP certificate or most recent third-party facility audit (NSF, UL, Eurofins, SGS, etc.). Amazon's verification (Gate B) is built on this; if a manufacturer can't produce it, the product may be unsellable on Amazon regardless of quality. Get the document *before* money moves.
2. **Finished-product COA** — confirm in writing that your run ships with a Certificate of Analysis covering identity, potency (label-claim verification), heavy metals, and micro. Ask whether that's included in the quote or extra.
3. **Amazon TIC experience** — ask directly: "Have you taken other brands through Amazon's third-party supplement verification, and can you supply the documentation package the TIC providers ask for?" A yes here saves you weeks.
4. **Label responsibility** — who signs off that the Supplement Facts panel is 21 CFR 101.36-compliant, you or them? Get it named in the paperwork either way, and run the Gate A check regardless.
5. **Lot coding & expiry** — confirm they print lot numbers and best-by dates, and how shelf life was assigned (needed for the COA and for Amazon's expiry requirements on FBA consumables).

None of this is adversarial — a good co-packer has this folder ready and answers in a day. Silence or vagueness on item 1 or 2 is the one red flag worth delaying production over.

### Gate B: Amazon's supplement verification (the long pole)
As of 2026 Amazon requires **all** dietary supplements on the US marketplace to be verified by an approved third-party Testing, Inspection & Certification (TIC) provider — proving cGMP manufacturing (21 CFR 111), label accuracy, and no contaminants. Approved providers include NSF, Eurofins, UL, Intertek, SGS, Certified Laboratories, and Mérieux. Non-compliance = listing suppressed.

Action this week (one email, 20 minutes):
1. Ask your co-packer for their **cGMP certificate / most recent third-party audit**, the **finished-product COA** for your run, and whether they've taken other brands through Amazon's TIC verification (good co-packers have a folder ready).
2. Get a quote from one TIC provider (Eurofins and Certified Laboratories tend to be the most small-brand-friendly). Budget **$1,500–3,000** and **4–8 weeks**. Start it in parallel with production — it's the item most likely to delay you.

## 3. The 8-week plan (~5 hrs/week)

| Week | Focus | Key actions |
|------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Gates | Label check ordered; co-packer docs requested; TIC quote requested; Brand Registry started (needs a trademark application — file intent-to-use for "herbody" now, ~$350 + optional attorney) |
| 2 | Account | Seller Central account (Professional, $39.99/mo); apply to sell in Dietary Supplements category; submit TIC paperwork |
| 3–4 | Listing | Write title/bullets (below); brief a designer for the 7-image stack (~$300–600 on Fiverr Pro/Upwork — this is your highest-ROI spend); keyword research with Helium 10 free tier |
| 5 | Logistics | Ship ~300–400 units to FBA (not all 1,300 — storage fees and risk); keep the rest at home/3PL |
| 6 | Launch | Listing live; enroll in **Amazon Vine** immediately (30 units, ~$200 fee — this is how you get your first ~20–30 reviews legitimately); turn on Subscribe & Save at 5–10% |
| 7–8 | Traffic | One auto PPC campaign + one exact-match campaign on 5–10 core keywords, $20–30/day cap; launch price ~15% below your target price, raise after ~50 reviews |

**Weekly rhythm once live (fits a lunch break):** 20 min PPC check twice a week (negate junk search terms, raise bids on converters), reply to questions/reviews, check inventory levels. That's the whole ongoing job.

## 4. Listing essentials (draft to react to)

**Title:**
`herbody All-in-One Daily Powder for Women — Creatine Monohydrate, Collagen Peptides, Complete Multivitamin, Myo-Inositol — Supports Energy, Strength, Hair Skin & Nails, Healthy Water Balance — Strawberry Raspberry, 30 Servings`

**Bullet skeleton (each kills one objection, in the order shoppers raise them):**
1. **EVERYTHING IN ONE SCOOP** — replaces 5+ separate supplements: creatine, collagen, full vitamins, inositol. One ritual, 30 seconds. *(objection: "is it worth it?" — do the cabinet-replacement math: bought separately these run $80+/mo)*
2. **CREATINE WITHOUT THE BLOAT** — X g creatine monohydrate paired with our water-balance botanical blend. *(the differentiator — lead the image stack with this too)*
3. **ACTUALLY TASTES GOOD** — real strawberry-raspberry, mixes clean in water, no chalky texture. *(taste is the #1 review-killer for powders)*
4. **MADE FOR WOMEN'S DAILY RHYTHM** — myo-inositol + methylated B-vitamins. *(identity bullet; keep claims structure/function)*
5. **TESTED AND TRANSPARENT** — third-party tested, cGMP manufactured, full-dose label (no fairy-dusting). *(trust bullet — you'll have the TIC verification to back it)*

**Image stack (in order):** hero pouch → "one scoop replaces this shelf" flat-lay → ingredient/dose infographic → "creatine without the bloat" explainer → taste/mixability shot → lifestyle (gym bag/desk) → Subscribe & Save close. The flat-lay of 6 bottles vs. your one pouch is your money image — it's the AG1 argument in a single frame.

## 5. The website: do the 4-hour version, not the 40-hour one

With 1,300 units, Amazon is your store; the website's only jobs are legitimacy (people Google you before buying) and owning emails for run #2.

- One-page Shopify (Basic, or even just Linkpop/Carrd at first): hero image, the one-liner, ingredient panel, FAQ, FDA disclaimer, **email capture** ("Founding member — 20% off launch"), and a *Buy on Amazon* button.
- Do **not** build checkout, subscriptions, blog, or reviews infrastructure yet. That's a post-validation project.
- The email list is the real asset: it's your day-one traffic for launch (external traffic that converts boosts Amazon rank) and your audience for run #2.

---

## 6. Charity-per-sale: the honest lightweight version

Right instinct, wrong time to build a program. Two things to know:

1. **Legal:** formally advertising "we donate $X per sale to [named charity]" makes you a *commercial co-venturer* in many US states — several require registration and a written contract with the charity. Not worth the overhead at 1,300 units.
2. **The low-effort version that's fully legit:** make a private pledge (e.g., 1% of revenue), donate quarterly yourself, and talk about it in past tense — "last quarter herbody donated $X to [charity]" with the receipt. No registration issues, no infrastructure, and it reads as more genuine than a badge. When you're ready to formalize, Shopify apps like **Pledge** or joining **1% for the Planet** handle the compliance for you.

Pick a cause tightly linked to the brand (women's health research, girls' sports access) — a random charity reads as marketing; a linked one compounds the positioning.

## 7. What NOT to do (your overwhelm shield)

- No TikTok content grind, no influencer outreach program, no ambassador scheme — not until reviews exist and the listing converts. One channel (Amazon) done properly beats four done at 20%.
- No full DTC site, no subscription infrastructure, no retail outreach.
- No paid ads outside Amazon PPC.
- Don't ship all 1,300 units to FBA (storage fees, and you lose flexibility).
- Don't buy reviews or use "review clubs" — with the new verification regime, supplement accounts are under extra scrutiny and this kills brands permanently. Vine + insert card with a QR to a thank-you page is the ceiling.
