# herbody — Amazon UK Launch Plan (1,300-unit first run)

A launch plan for a solo founder with a full-time job, launching on **Amazon UK**. Built for roughly **5 focused hours per week**. The theme throughout: with 1,300 units, this run's job is to *validate*, not to scale. Spend money and hours only on things that directly cause a sale or protect the launch.

---

## 1. The positioning call (make this once, it shapes everything)

"Be like AG1" is the right ambition but the wrong playbook for launch. AG1 grew on podcasts, founder authority, and DTC subscriptions — a model that takes years and millions. What you *can* take from AG1 is the **belief system**: "replace your shelf of bottles with one daily ritual."

Your version, sharpened for women and for Amazon search:

> **herbody — the all-in-one daily for women. Creatine, collagen, full-spectrum vitamins and inositol in one strawberry-raspberry scoop.**

Comparable brands to study (not copy): Bloom Nutrition (women's greens, TikTok-native), Free Soul (UK women's wellness — your closest UK reference brand), Innermost, Motion Nutrition, Wild Nutrition. Your gap: nobody owns "women's all-in-one **with a full creatine dose**" on Amazon UK yet — greens powders sprinkle it or skip it.

### ⚠️ The UK claims reality (this is the biggest difference from a US launch)

The UK does not allow US-style "supports X" language. On pack and in your listing you may only use **authorised health claims from the GB Nutrition and Health Claims Register**, worded closely to the register entry. What that means for your exact formula:

- **Creatine** ✓ — authorised claim: *"Creatine increases physical performance in successive bursts of short-term, high-intensity exercise"* — but only if the daily dose delivers **3 g**. If your formula is under 3 g/day, you cannot make this claim. Check the spec now; if you're at 2–2.5 g, ask Black Label whether bumping to 3 g is possible before production, because it unlocks your single strongest legal claim.
- **Collagen** ✗ — there are **no authorised health claims for collagen** in GB. The standard, fully legal workaround: your formula almost certainly contains vitamin C, which carries the claim *"Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin."* That sentence is how every compliant UK collagen brand markets — use it verbatim.
- **Inositol** ✗ — no authorised claims. You can name it and state the dose, but attach no benefit language.
- **"Diuretic" / bloating** ✗✗ — this is your highest-risk element in the UK. "Reduces bloating/water retention" is an unauthorised health claim, and presenting a product as a *diuretic* can push it toward being classed as a medicine by the MHRA. Do not print the word, and drop "without the bloat" as a claim on pack or in the listing. You can still name the botanicals and their amounts. (In organic social content the enforcement reality is looser, but keep the label and Amazon listing clean.)
- **Your workhorse claims** come from the vitamins and minerals: B vitamins (*"contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"*, *"normal energy-yielding metabolism"*), biotin/zinc (*"contributes to the maintenance of normal hair/skin"*), and the vitamin C/collagen-formation claim. General claims like "wellness" or "feel your best" are allowed only when accompanied by a specific authorised claim.

This isn't a niche technicality — Amazon UK's supplement review and Trading Standards both check claims, and 1,300 printed pouches with an illegal claim are unsellable.

## 2. Week 0 — the gates before anything else

### Gate A: label compliance check — BEFORE the pouch prints
Artwork finalized but unprinted is the single luckiest fact in your situation. Have a UK food-law consultant (£300–500; search "food supplement label compliance UK" — firms like Ashbury and several solo regulatory consultants do fixed-price reviews) verify against the **Food Supplements (England) Regulations 2003** and **assimilated Regulation (EU) 1169/2011**:

- The words **"food supplement"** on pack (mandatory designation)
- Recommended daily portion + **"Do not exceed the recommended daily dose"**
- **"Food supplements should not be used as a substitute for a varied diet"**
- **"Keep out of reach of young children"**
- Nutrient amounts **per daily dose** with **% NRV** for vitamins/minerals
- Allergens emphasised in the ingredients list (bold)
- Your **FBO name and UK address**, net quantity, best-before, lot code space
- Every claim on pack checked against the GB register (see §1)
- No "diuretic", no weight-loss language, no disease references

### Gate A½: settle these with Black Label Creations BEFORE agreeing payment terms
You're waiting on payment terms — that's leverage, and it disappears the moment you pay. Fold these into one email this week:

1. **GMP/HACCP proof** — ask for their current GMP certificate or GFSI-benchmarked certification (BRCGS, SALSA, ISO 22000) and confirmation they're a registered food business. Amazon UK asks for exactly these documents when you apply to list supplements; if a manufacturer can't produce them, the product may be unsellable on Amazon regardless of quality. Get the documents *before* money moves.
2. **Finished-product COA** — confirm in writing that your run ships with a Certificate of Analysis covering identity, label-claim potency, heavy metals, and micro — and whether it's included in the quote or extra.
3. **Amazon UK experience** — ask directly: "Have you supplied the compliance pack (GMP cert, COA, spec sheets) for brands selling on Amazon UK, and can you provide it for us?" A yes saves you weeks.
4. **Label responsibility** — who signs off that the label meets the 2003 Regulations and the claims register, you or them? Get it named in the paperwork either way, and run Gate A regardless.
5. **Creatine dose** — confirm the daily dose; if under 3 g, ask the cost/feasibility of hitting 3 g (see §1 — it unlocks your strongest claim).
6. **Lot coding & best-before** — confirm they print both and how shelf life was assigned.

None of this is adversarial — a good UK co-packer has this folder ready and answers in a day. Silence or vagueness on item 1 or 2 is the one red flag worth delaying production over.

### Gate B: register as a Food Business Operator (free, but do it now)
To sell food supplements in the UK you must register as a food business with your **local council** — it's a free online form, but must be done **at least 28 days before trading**. Home-based businesses register at their home address. Amazon UK asks for evidence of FBO status when approving supplement listings. Twenty minutes, zero cost, often forgotten until it blocks the listing.

### Gate C: Amazon UK category approval
Supplements are a gated category on amazon.co.uk. When you apply, expect to submit: product label images (all sides), the manufacturer's GMP/HACCP/GFSI certificate, a COA, your FBO details, and an EAN barcode (buy a legitimate one from **GS1 UK**, not a barcode reseller — Amazon rejects resold ranges). This is document assembly, not lab testing — cheaper and faster than the US equivalent, typically days-to-a-couple-of-weeks once your paperwork is complete.

## 3. The 8-week plan (~5 hrs/week)

| Week | Focus | Key actions |
|------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Gates | Label review commissioned; Black Label email sent; FBO registration filed; **UKIPO trademark** application for "herbody" (~£170, enables Amazon Brand Registry); GS1 UK barcode |
| 2 | Account | Seller Central UK account (Professional, £25/mo + VAT); begin supplements category approval with the document pack from Black Label |
| 3–4 | Listing | Write title/bullets (below); brief a designer for the 7-image stack (£250–500 — your highest-ROI spend); keyword research with Helium 10 free tier on the UK marketplace |
| 5 | Logistics | Ship ~300–400 units to FBA UK (not all 1,300 — storage fees and risk); keep the rest at home |
| 6 | Launch | Listing live; enrol in **Amazon Vine** immediately (~£170 + 30 units — your first ~20–30 legitimate reviews); turn on Subscribe & Save at 5–10% |
| 7–8 | Traffic | One auto PPC campaign + one exact-match campaign on 5–10 core UK keywords, £15–25/day cap; launch price ~15% below target, raise after ~50 reviews |

**VAT note:** food supplements are standard-rated at 20% in the UK. You don't have to register for VAT until £90k turnover, but price your margin as if VAT applies — if run #2 scales you'll cross into it, and a price built without VAT headroom breaks later.

**Weekly rhythm once live (fits a lunch break):** 20 min PPC check twice a week (negate junk search terms, raise bids on converters), reply to questions/reviews, check inventory. That's the whole ongoing job.

## 4. Listing essentials (draft to react to — claims-safe for the UK)

**Title:**
`herbody All-in-One Daily Powder for Women — Creatine Monohydrate, Collagen Peptides, Complete Multivitamin, Myo-Inositol — with Vitamin C which Contributes to Normal Collagen Formation — Strawberry Raspberry, 30 Servings`

**Bullet skeleton (each kills one objection, in claims-safe language):**
1. **EVERYTHING IN ONE SCOOP** — replaces 5+ separate supplements: creatine, collagen, a complete multivitamin and myo-inositol. One ritual, 30 seconds. *(objection: "worth it?" — bought separately these run £60+/month)*
2. **A FULL DOSE, NOT A SPRINKLE** — X g creatine monohydrate per serving. *(if ≥3 g, add the authorised performance claim verbatim; if not, state the dose only)*
3. **BEAUTY FROM WITHIN, DONE PROPERLY** — collagen peptides plus vitamin C, which contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin. *(the legal collagen play)*
4. **FIGHT THE 3PM SLUMP** — B6 and B12 contribute to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue. *(authorised claim doing emotional work)*
5. **ACTUALLY TASTES GOOD** — real strawberry-raspberry, mixes clean in water, no chalky texture. *(taste is the #1 review-killer for powders)*

**Image stack (in order):** hero pouch → "one scoop replaces this shelf" flat-lay of six bottles vs. your pouch (the AG1 argument in a single frame — your money image) → ingredient/dose infographic with %NRVs → GMP-manufactured/third-party-tested trust badges → taste/mixability shot → lifestyle (gym bag/desk) → Subscribe & Save close.

## 5. The website: do the 4-hour version, not the 40-hour one

With 1,300 units, Amazon is your store; the website's only jobs are legitimacy (people Google you before buying) and owning emails for run #2.

- One-page Shopify (Basic, or even Carrd at first): hero image, the one-liner, ingredient panel, FAQ, the mandatory food-supplement statements, **email capture** ("Founding member — 20% off launch"), and a *Buy on Amazon* button.
- Do **not** build checkout, subscriptions, blog, or reviews infrastructure yet. Post-validation project.
- The email list is the real asset: day-one launch traffic (external traffic that converts boosts Amazon rank) and your audience for run #2.
- Same claims rules apply to your website and social captions — Trading Standards and the ASA both enforce there.

## 6. Charity-per-sale: the honest lightweight version

Right instinct, wrong time to build a program. In the UK, advertising "we donate £X per sale to [named charity]" makes you a **commercial participator** under the Charities Act 1992 — legally requiring a written agreement with the charity and a prescribed statement wherever you advertise it. Not worth the overhead at 1,300 units.

The low-effort version that's fully legit: make a private pledge (e.g., 1% of revenue), donate quarterly yourself, and talk about it in past tense — "last quarter herbody donated £X to [charity]" with the receipt. No agreements needed, and it reads as more genuine than a badge. When you're ready to formalise, **Work for Good** is a UK platform built exactly for this — it handles the commercial participator agreement for small businesses.

Pick a cause tightly linked to the brand (women's health research, girls' sport access) — a random charity reads as marketing; a linked one compounds the positioning.

## 7. What NOT to do (your overwhelm shield)

- No TikTok content grind, no influencer outreach programme, no ambassador scheme — not until reviews exist and the listing converts. One channel done properly beats four done at 20%.
- No full DTC site, no subscription infrastructure, no retail outreach, no EU expansion (that's a separate compliance regime now).
- No paid ads outside Amazon PPC.
- Don't ship all 1,300 units to FBA (storage fees, lost flexibility).
- Don't buy reviews or use "review clubs" — supplement accounts get extra scrutiny and this kills brands permanently. Vine + an insert card with a QR to a thank-you page is the ceiling.
