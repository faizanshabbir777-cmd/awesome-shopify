01 · Foundation

Philosophy, positioning, audience, voice

The philosophy

her. one scoop.
Five things she already buys, at the doses the studies used, in one honest ritual. We don't sell her a promise. We hand her the finished routine.

  • Completeness over cleverness. The product's truth is that it finishes the job; every surface says "complete" before it says anything else.
  • Doses, not adjectives. Numbers on the front. "Premium", "advanced", "enhancer" never appear. Proof furniture (grams, counts, marks) replaces claims.
  • Adult, addressed, unhurried. She is spoken to, in the second person, in sentences that start with "her". No exclamation marks. No pink-and-shrink.
  • Evidence, translated. The science is shown as "in studies of the ingredient", charted honestly, and never inflated into a claim the UK register doesn't allow.
  • One ritual. The scoop is the object, the mark and the habit. Everything points at 7am.

Positioning

For · who · is · that · unlike · because

For women 35–55 (and the 24–34 who follow them) who are buying creatine, collagen, a multivitamin and inositol as four separate products and want the edge back — energy, memory, shape — without the bro aisle or the sprinkle-dose "for her" tubs, herbody Body Beauty is the complete daily scoop that delivers all four at the doses the research used, unlike Free Soul, Wellgard, Warrior and Bloom, which each sell one or two of them, because 5g creatine · 5g collagen · a full multivitamin · myo-inositol is on the front of the pack and in every scoop.

The enemy

Not a brand — a behaviour: the £59 shelf of half-finished tubs, and the pink products that charge more for 3g. Every asset either replaces the shelf or exposes the sprinkle.

Two buyers, one pack

Core · 35–55 · "steady & sharp"

Perimenopause-aware, strength-training on advice, reads labels, trusts named experts and other women her age. Motivations in the data: cognition > energy > strength; "muscle" is eighth. Price-tolerant when the dose is honest. Anxiety she won't say aloud: losing the edge.

Growth · 24–34 · "sorted"

Gym-curious, feed-fed, already buys collagen and a multi, tired of five tubs. Converts on taste, aesthetics, per-day price and creators. Wants the pouch to look good on the counter. Anxiety: bloat, breakouts, "am I doing this right".

Voice

Do

"5g, because that's the dose the research uses." · "her body. her rules. her scoop." · "Creatine isn't a gym supplement. It's a women's-health one." · Short sentences. Second person. Specific numbers. Full stops.

Don't

"Unlock your glow-up!" · "Snatched by summer" · "Clinically formulated for optimal recomposition" · "ladies", "girlboss", "hack", "detox", "balance your hormones". No exclamation marks, no lab voice, no youth slang.

Name architecture

herbody (house) → BODY BEAUTY (product) → "the complete daily" (descriptor). Future SKUs keep the two-word shape (Body Calm, Body Bright). Never "Her-" prefixes, "-Glow" suffixes, "Crea-" prefixes — those are the category's crowded lanes.

03 · Distinctive assets

Three things we own. Never vary them.

Distinctive-asset practice (Romaniuk/Sharp): pick about three assets, always present them together so they wire together in memory, and never let them drift — "every inconsistency actively prevents assets from gaining fame". A small brand needs far less exposure to build them than a big one, provided it is relentlessly consistent.

Iconography

One style only: single-weight line icons (1.5pt at print scale) in black cherry, used sparingly on the back panel and A+ (a scoop, a glass, a clock for "daily"). No flat-vector-meets-photoreal mixing, no pictogram sets.

Photography

  • Still life: pouch and copper scoop on stone/marble, morning side-light, warm-neutral grade, raspberry only in the powder and the fruit.
  • Sensory: the pink pour in slow motion, cut strawberries and raspberries, condensation on glass — this is the appetite the cream pack needs.
  • People: women 35–55 in movement, laughing mid-sentence, hands and shoulders more than faces; black-and-white with a plum duotone, product in colour. No gym-mirror selfies, no before/after.
  • Never: pink pastel sets, rose-gold props, spa imagery, lab coats.

Motion

The her. chip is the "hero frame" of a two-beat animation: the full stop lands last. The scoop glyph fills as a loader. The raspberry pour is the only slow-motion asset. No particle effects, no gradients in motion.

Back panel — required contents

  • Product description (one paragraph, brand voice, no unauthorised claims)
  • Full ingredients list, descending order, allergens in bold; "contains bovine collagen"

Never on pack, listing, site, or paid social

  • Outcome words without an authorised claim beside them: glow, tone, sculpt, recomposition, enhancer, anti-ageing, lean, snatched.
  • Disease / medicinal: PCOS, fertility, hormone balance, menopause relief, perimenopause support, cortisol, insulin, diuretic, water retention, de-bloat, bloating, brain fog, anxiety, depression, bone density.
  • Collagen / HA / inositol benefits stated of the ingredient itself — all three have no authorised claim.
  • "Clinically proven" — requires a trial on the finished product. Use "in clinical studies of the ingredient".
  • Any "% of women fear bloating" statistic — no peer-reviewed survey exists; do not print one.
  • Blend or product names that imply a benefit are themselves health claims under Regulation 1924/2006 and must sit beside an authorised claim.

11 · AI workflow

How to use image models without getting the category average

  • Assets first, adjectives never. Prompt the specific asset (the chip, the glyph, the four-cell strip, the palette by hex). "Premium", "unique", "modern" produce the average pouch.
  • Don't trust generated text. Image models still garble copy. Generate the pouch with placeholder blocks, then set all type in Figma/Illustrator on the dieline. Ideogram-class tools are acceptable for concept text only.
  • Generators output RGB. Nothing they make is print-ready; the dieline file is rebuilt as vector with Pantone and CMYK.
  • Thumbnail every concept. 64px on white beside a pink competitor, every round, before anyone falls in love with a render.
  • Reject non-producible outputs. Models propose embossed film, impossible windows and materials that don't exist at this volume. Check §05 and §10 before presenting.
