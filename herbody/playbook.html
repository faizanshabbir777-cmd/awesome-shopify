Sell the shelf,
not the scoop.

AG1 didn't win by having the best greens — it won by standing for one belief: your cluttered supplement shelf is the enemy, and one ritual replaces it. This playbook applies that belief to a market AG1 ignores: women who want creatine, collagen and their vitamins without buying five products — and without the bro-science aisle.

01 · The wedge

Win one search, not a category

The greens-powder shelf on Amazon UK is a graveyard for new brands: hundreds of listings, review counts in the tens of thousands, PPC bids priced for venture money. You don't have the units or the hours to fight there. But there's a wedge sitting open:

The positioning

Women's creatine is exploding, and every product serving it is either a bro brand in pink or a wellness powder with a fairy-dust sprinkle. herbody is the only all-in-one built around a full creatine dose — plus the collagen, vitamins and inositol she's already buying separately.

Strategically this means you enter through "creatine for women" and its long tail — a search term with fast-growing volume, weak incumbents, and buyers who already know what they want — rather than "greens powder" or "multivitamin," where you'd be invisible. The all-in-one story then does the upsell work inside the listing: she came for creatine, she stays because it replaces four other purchases.

Name the enemy

Not a competitor — a behaviour: the £70 shelf of half-finished tubs and the pink-tax products that charge more for less. Every piece of copy either attacks the cluttered shelf or the sprinkle-dose.

One belief, everywhere

"One scoop replaces the shelf" is the thesis of the hero image, the first bullet, the website headline and the insert card. Repetition is the strategy — 1,300 units can't afford two messages.

03 · Price architecture

Charge for the shelf you replace

Price against the basket she's already buying, not against other powders. Creatine (£12) + collagen (£20) + multivitamin (£12) + inositol (£15) is a £59/month habit before shipping. That's your anchor, and it belongs in the images, not just the copy.

  • Subscribe & Save from day one (5–10%). A daily powder is a subscription product wearing a pouch. S&S attach rate is also your single best signal that the product — not the discount — is working.
  • Don't price under £30. Below that you read as another sprinkle product, and you can't fund PPC out of margin. The formula justifies the premium; the anchor image proves it.
  • Hold one lever back: a 2-pouch bundle at a visible saving is your week-6 move if velocity stalls — not a launch-day giveaway.

05 · Launch sequence

Manufacture momentum you don't have

Amazon's algorithm rewards early velocity and conversion. A cold launch wastes the honeymoon; a choreographed one compounds it. The sequence matters more than any single tactic:

  1. Build a founding list while production runs

    Now → launch

    One-page site, one job: "Founding member — 20% off launch week." Push it through your personal network, her group chats, local gym noticeboards. Fifty warm emails is enough; a hundred is a weapon. This costs time you have in tiny fragments, not money.

  2. Seed reviews before you need them

    Listing live, week 1

    Enrol 30 units in Amazon Vine the day the listing goes live. Reviews are the whole game at your scale: organically ~2–4% of buyers review, so 1,300 units yields maybe 30–50 reviews unaided. Vine front-loads the first 20–30 so real shoppers never see a zero-review listing.

  3. Fire the list in a 72-hour window

    Launch week

    Send the founding-member email in one concentrated burst, at the ladder price. A spike of external, high-converting traffic in a tight window is the strongest ranking signal a small brand can buy — and yours is free.

  4. Let PPC discover, then concentrate

    Weeks 1–4

    One auto campaign (£10–15/day) as a search-term listening post; one exact campaign on your 5 wedge keywords (£10–15/day). Weekly, move converting terms from auto into exact and negate the junk. That 20-minute loop is your ad strategy — resist adding campaigns.

  5. Convert buyers into the flywheel

    Ongoing

    Insert card in every pouch: QR to a "make it work" page — how to dose, what week 3 feels like, then a nudge to review and to S&S. A customer who uses the product properly gets results; results write your 5-star reviews for run two.

  6. Spend the win, not the hope

    Week 6+

    Only after the listing converts: raise price to target, deploy the 2-pouch bundle if velocity dips, and then consider seeding 10–15 pouches to UK nano-creators in the women's-lifting niche for UGC you can license into the listing. Content amplifies a listing that works; it cannot save one that doesn't.

06 · The scorecard

1,300 units is an experiment — grade it like one

This run's real product is information. Decide now what the data must show, so run two is a decision instead of a mood. Judge it at day 90:

Green — reorder bigger

The wedge is real

Listing conversion ≥10%, S&S attach ≥15%, taste praised in reviews, ad spend ≤25% of sales. Reorder 3–5k units, raise price, start the creator seeding.

Amber — fix, don't scale

Traffic yes, conversion no

Clicks come but conversion <8%: it's price, images or reviews — not the product. Rework the stack and offer before spending another ad pound.

Red — reposition

The wedge missed

Weak clicks on wedge terms or taste complaints in reviews. Sell through at cost via the list, keep the audience, relaunch with the formula or angle the reviews demand.

Track four numbers weekly, ignore the rest: sessions, conversion rate, TACOS (total ad spend ÷ total sales), and S&S attach. Everything you need to know about run two lives in those four.

07 · The founder's five hours

An operating system for a full-time-job launch

Two fixed blocks

Tue + Thu, 20 min: PPC loop — harvest converters, negate junk, check the four numbers. Sat/Sun, 2 hrs: the week's single project (image brief, email draft, insert card). One project per week, finished, beats four started.

Ruthless nos (for now)

No TikTok posting schedule, no influencer programme, no full DTC store, no retail outreach, no second flavour, no EU. Every one of these is a run-two decision that gets easier with run-one data — and impossible without it.

Guardrails — the short version

Strategy only works if the words are legal

  • Creatine's power claim needs 3g/day. Confirm the dose with Black Label before production — at 3g you can legally say it "increases physical performance in short bursts of high-intensity exercise." Under 3g, you can only state the amount. This one spec decision is worth real conversion.
  • Collagen has no approved UK claim — the legal play every UK brand uses: vitamin C "contributes to normal collagen formation for normal skin." Say that; imply the rest with imagery.
  • Never print "diuretic" or "reduces bloating." Name the botanicals and doses; let the ingredient list speak. B-vitamins carry your energy language ("reduction of tiredness and fatigue").
  • The unglamorous trio — label review before printing, free food-business registration with your council, and Black Label's GMP certificate for Amazon's category approval — is covered in the full plan in your repo. Boring, cheap, non-negotiable.
